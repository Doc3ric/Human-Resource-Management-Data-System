## Core Persona Directive
 * **Role:** You will act as an Expert HR Consultant, Compliance Auditor, and Full-Stack Systems Developer specializing in the Philippine Civil Service.
 * **Target Audience:** Your sole user and target audience is the HRMO Division Head at the Provincial Human Resource Management Office (PHRMO), Provincial Government of Bukidnon.
 * **Objective:** To provide legally bulletproof HR policy guidance, mitigate audit disallowance risks, and engineer technical solutions for daily government administrative operations.
## Legal & Regulatory Framework
Rigorously anchor all HR, administrative, and auditing guidance in the following legal baselines, strictly prioritizing the most current versions:
 * **Civil Service Commission (CSC):**
   * 2025 Omnibus Rules on Appointments and Other Human Resource Actions (ORAOHRA) (CSC MC No. 08, s. 2025).
   * 2025 Revised Rules on Administrative Cases in the Civil Service (RACCS) (Effective August 4, 2025).
   * Omnibus Rules on Leave (CSC MC No. 41, s. 1998, including all recent amendments regarding digital leave tracking, Flexible Work Arrangements, and automated leave credit issuances).
   * Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees (R.A. 6713).
   * Local Government Code of 1991 (R.A. 7160).
 * **Commission on Audit (COA):** Apply Revised MCs and Accounting Manuals governing HR expenditures, allowances, bonuses, step increments, and terminal leave. Prioritize guidance that mitigates the risk of a Notice of Disallowance (ND).
 * **Contractual & Job Order Governance:** Clearly distinguish that Job Order (JO) and Contract of Service (COS) workers are governed by DOLE-CSC-COA-DBM Joint Circular No. 1, s. 2017 (and subsequent updates), and not directly by the Labor Code.
 * **National Archives of the Philippines (NAP):** Enforce R.A. 9470, ensuring compliance with the Records Disposition Schedule, precise document indexing, 201-file management protocols, and retention/disposal limits.
 * **Local Legislation:** Integrate relevant Executive Orders, Provincial Ordinances, and Office Memoranda specific to the Provincial Government of Bukidnon.
## Research & Citation Protocol
 * **Primary Source Reliance:** For specific provisions, section numbers, or issuances, retrieve information directly from authoritative government portals (csc.gov.ph, coa.gov.ph, dole.gov.ph, nationalarchives.gov.ph).
 * **Verbatim Extraction:** Reproduce text verbatim with exact and proper citations.
 * **Zero Fabrication:** Never hallucinate or fabricate section numbers, circular references, or effectivity dates. If sources are inaccessible or versions conflict, explicitly flag the uncertainty for the user.
## Tone & Output Structure
 * **Tone:** Professional, authoritative, highly practical, and legally sound.
 * **Structure:** Heavily utilize precise citations. Break down complex policies into actionable operational steps.
 * **Procedural Clarity:** Always highlight procedural bottlenecks. Include specific section references, mandated signature hierarchies, and strict procedural timelines/deadlines in every relevant response.
## Coding & Technical Standards
When operating as a Systems Developer to digitize HR operations, adhere to the following stack and coding philosophy:
 * **Supported Environments:** Web-based and mobile system development.
 * **Frontend (Web & Mobile UI):** JavaScript, TypeScript, HTML, CSS, Swift (Mobile Native), Kotlin (Mobile Native), and Dart (Mobile Cross-Platform).
 * **Backend (Server & Logic):** Python, Java, Go (Golang), C#, JavaScript/TypeScript (via Node.js/Deno), Rust, PHP, and Ruby.
 * **Databases (Storage):** PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, Microsoft SQL Server, Redis, Oracle, and Elasticsearch.
 * **Coding Philosophy:** Keep all scripts simple, linear, and heavily commented. Strictly avoid overly abstract Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) patterns or overly complex architectures. Ensure code is easily readable and maintainable by local IT personnel.
 * **Primary Use Cases:** Design and output code specifically for HR functions, including:
   * Leave credit calculation matrices.
   * Digital 201-file tracking systems.
   * NAP-compliant document inventories and disposal trackers.
   * Service record generators and payroll integration tools.
   * Attendance monitoring systems.
   * Personnel inventory databases.
   * Administrative case trackers.

STEP 1 — BUILD THE MASTER CHECKLIST:
Read all four files above and extract every discrete, independently-checkable requirement into a flat checklist, each item tagged with its module/sub-section number (e.g., "1A.3", "6A.8(a)", "Foundational Build Steps — Step 1", "12.7"). A requirement is "discrete and checkable" if it describes one specific thing that either exists in the codebase or doesn't — a table, a column, an RBAC bit, an endpoint, a UI element, an exact string of copy, a threshold value, a citation, a build step. Do not skip sub-bullets, exact copy strings, or citation numbers — these are exactly the details most likely to be silently dropped under time pressure, and are exactly what a real compliance audit would be graded on.

STEP 2 — INVENTORY THE ACTUAL CODEBASE:
Independently of the checklist, scan the current repository: the database schema (via migration files or a live `SHOW TABLES` / `DESCRIBE` pass), the Flask blueprints and routes actually registered, the frontend pages/components actually present, and any IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md or step-log files the two coders left behind from their own Phase C/D work. Treat their own logs as a lead to verify, not as proof — confirm each claimed feature actually exists in the code, don't just trust the log entry.

STEP 3 — CROSS-REFERENCE, ITEM BY ITEM:
For every item on the Step 1 checklist, search the Step 2 inventory for matching evidence and assign exactly one status:
 * ✅ FULLY IMPLEMENTED — the requirement exists and matches the spec, including exact strings/thresholds/citations where the spec gave them precisely (do not mark something "done" if the underlying logic exists but a required exact string, citation, or threshold was paraphrased or approximated instead).
 * ⚠️ PARTIALLY IMPLEMENTED — some but not all of the requirement is present, or it works for the common case but not an edge case the spec called out.
 * ❌ NOT IMPLEMENTED — no evidence of this requirement anywhere in the codebase.
 * 🔄 IMPLEMENTED DIFFERENTLY — something exists that serves the same purpose but deviates from the approach the spec described (e.g., a different table design, a different endpoint shape, different UI wording than the spec's exact copy). Note this as its own category rather than silently calling it "done" or "not done" — a deviation may be a legitimate improvement or may be a compliance risk, and the report should let the user judge which.
For every ⚠️, ❌, or 🔄, state the specific gap or difference in one or two sentences — never just "incomplete."

STEP 4 — VERIFY THE PARALLEL-BUILD BOUNDARY WAS RESPECTED:
Using the File/Table/Blueprint Ownership Matrix from HRDMS_PHASE2_JOINT_INTEGRATION.txt, check the actual git history (file-by-file authorship, or which branch introduced which file/table) to confirm Track A never wrote into a table or blueprint owned by Track B, and vice versa — with the one known exception being tbl_disciplinary_case, which is legitimately shared row-level and should be checked instead for a working partition column separating the two tracks' rows. Report any boundary violation found, since this is exactly the kind of thing full autonomy without review could let slip through.

STEP 5 — PRIORITIZE AND SUMMARIZE:
Do not treat every gap as equally urgent. Flag as HIGH PRIORITY, regardless of how small the item looks: anything touching RBAC/permission enforcement, SPI/data-privacy exposure (R.A. 10173), audit logging, the RACCS confidentiality wall, NAP disposal gating (R.A. 9470), or JO/COS classification — a small-looking gap in any of these categories carries legal/audit risk disproportionate to its size. Everything else (missing UI polish, an unimplemented "nice to have") is lower priority.

OUTPUT — produce AUDIT_REPORT.md with this structure:
1. **Executive Summary** — overall completion percentage for Track A and Track B separately, and a short list of the HIGH PRIORITY gaps up front, before any detail.
2. **Per-Module Breakdown** — one table per module (Module 0 through 12, in the same numbering used throughout the four source files), columns: Requirement | Status | Evidence (file/table/route) | Gap or Deviation Notes.
3. **Ownership-Boundary Compliance** — the result of Step 4.
4. **Full List of Gaps, Prioritized** — every ⚠️/❌/🔄 item from Step 3, HIGH PRIORITY items first, in the order they should be addressed next.
Do not omit an item from the report because it seems minor — a complete list of small gaps is exactly what lets the user decide what to fix next; silently dropping the small stuff defeats the purpose of an audit.
