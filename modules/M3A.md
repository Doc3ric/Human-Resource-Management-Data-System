MODULE 3A — INCIDENT REPORT RECORDING WITH AI-ASSISTED CITATION & COUNSELING SUPPORT
Additive. Captures incident reports, provides an AI/rules-ASSISTED advisory draft of applicable violation citation, possible penalty range, and recommended counseling/improvement action, then routes for human review. CORE SAFEGUARD: the AI/rules output is an ADVISORY DRAFT ONLY, clearly labeled as such, never a determination or finding. A designated officer (per RBAC) reviews, edits, and owns every output. The system assists; it never adjudicates. This protects due process under the 2025 RACCS and avoids COA/appeal exposure from automated disciplinary action.
3A.1 Incident capture (basic information): a structured intake form recording — incident reference number (auto), date/time of incident, date reported, location, reporting party (name/role), person(s) involved (link to personnel_id where applicable), witnesses, incident category (e.g., attendance-related, conduct, performance, safety, property, interpersonal, other), a free-text factual narrative (what happened, observable facts only), immediate action taken, and attachments (photos, documents, statements) ingested through the IDCC pipeline. Capture facts, not conclusions; the narrative field prompts for observable facts and avoids pre-judging intent.
3A.2 AI/rules-assisted advisory draft: on submission of the facts, an assist engine produces a clearly-labeled ADVISORY DRAFT containing: (a) suggested applicable provision(s) — candidate citations from the 2025 RACCS (and related issuances) that the described facts may fall under, each shown with the rule/section and offense classification (light/less grave/grave) and a confidence/relevance note; (b) possible penalty RANGE for each candidate provision and offense tier (e.g., reprimand → suspension → dismissal ladders), pulled from the rules table, presented as a range not a verdict; (c) recommended counseling/improvement action — constructive, developmental suggestions (e.g., coaching session, performance improvement plan, expectation-setting, referral to employee wellness) aimed at correction and improvement rather than punishment. Implementation of the assist engine (on-premise local model vs deterministic rules-based mapping of facts→provisions; no external API for SPI) is decided in Phase A; either is acceptable provided no SPI leaves the LAN. Every draft is watermarked/labeled: "ADVISORY DRAFT — AI/RULES-ASSISTED. NOT A FINDING OR PENALTY. For review and determination by the authorized officer under 2025 RACCS due process."
3A.3 Mandatory human review & ownership: the advisory draft routes to the authorized reviewing officer (per RBAC) who must review, may edit any citation/penalty/recommendation, and must explicitly accept or override before anything is finalized. The system records the officer's edits and final decision, the original AI/rules draft, and the delta between them (so it is auditable what the human changed). Nothing is recorded as an official action until a human finalizes. The reviewing officer's identity, decision, and timestamp are stored and audit-logged.
3A.4 Dual track — counseling vs RACCS escalation: classify each finalized incident as either (a) LIGHT/DEVELOPMENTAL — stays in this module as a counseling/coaching record with the recommended improvement action, a counseling-session log (date, attendees, agreed actions, follow-up date), and a non-punitive tone; or (b) GRAVE/FORMAL — escalates into the formal disciplinary process and, where confidential, into the RACCS Confidentiality Wall (Module 9 Stage 5 / Module 10 M3) with all its air-gap, MFA, and access-logging protections. The reviewing officer (not the AI) sets the track; the system may suggest the track based on the candidate offense classification but the human confirms. Counseling-track records carry a lighter retention and are NOT treated as administrative penalties; escalated records follow RACCS handling and retention.
3A.5 Counseling & improvement support (developmental focus): for the counseling track, provide a structured counseling record: the constructive recommendation, an agreed improvement/action plan with target dates, a session log, and follow-up scheduling with reminders to the supervisor/HRMO. The emphasis is correction, support, and improvement (consistent with employee-wellbeing and progressive-discipline principles), not punishment. Where appropriate, surface referral options (wellness, training). This track aims to resolve issues before they become formal cases.
3A.6 Recording & audit: a finalized incident is recorded via the IDCC/Records pipeline (tbl_doc_metadata, doc_type e.g. INCIDENT-REPORT or INCIDENT-COUNSELING / INCIDENT-ESCALATED), linked to involved personnel_id(s) with strict privacy handling — incident records often contain SPI and sensitive content, so apply privacy_tier and, for escalated/confidential matters, the RACCS wall. All access is logged to tbl_audit_log (and tbl_spi_access_log where SPI). Light/counseling records are access-restricted to the involved supervisor, HRMO, and the employee as appropriate; grave/escalated records inherit RACCS access gating.
3A.7 RBAC (configurable): who may CREATE and who may FINALIZE an incident report is fully configurable via the RBAC matrix (Module 4A) — e.g., supervisors create while HRMO finalizes, or HRMO-only, or Department Head + HRMO co-approval, set per the office's policy through permission bits (incident_create, incident_review, incident_finalize, incident_escalate). The AI/rules assist is available to creators/reviewers but only holders of incident_finalize may finalize, and only incident_escalate may push to the RACCS track. Self-reporting and conflict-of-interest: a user cannot finalize an incident in which they are an involved party.
3A.8 Compliance & fairness safeguards: the advisory label is mandatory and non-removable on all AI/rules output; the system never imposes a penalty; due-process sequencing (notice, opportunity to be heard) is preserved for any escalation; the employee's right to respond is captured; the AI draft, human edits, and final decision are all retained for auditability and appeal. Tooltips cite 2025 RACCS (offense classifications and due process), R.A. 6713 (Code of Conduct), and R.A. 10173 (handling of sensitive incident data). Note that AI/rules suggestions are decision-support only and carry no legal authority; the authorized officer's determination under the 2025 RACCS governs.
